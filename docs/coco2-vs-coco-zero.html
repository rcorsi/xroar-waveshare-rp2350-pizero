CoCo 2 vs CoCo Zero

The same Color BASIC you remember. Everything around it, fixed.

Tandy CoCo 2

1983, tired, beloved

CoCo Zero

2026, on a 65 x 30 mm board

Picture
✕RF out to channel 3 or 4: fuzz, shimmer and a fight with the TV's tuner.
Picture
✓A sharp digital picture at 60 frames a second, with the NTSC artifact colours recreated (F8 swaps them per game).
Sound
✕A 6-bit DAC squeezed through the same RF signal, so the picture's buzz comes along for free.
Sound
✓Clean digital sound over the same one cable, plus the Games Master Cartridge's three-voice sound chip built in.
Speed
✕POKE 65497,0 doubles the speed and scrambles the screen.
Speed
✓Both high-speed POKEs work, up to twice as fast, and the screen stays clean at full speed.
Storage
✕Cassette tapes at 1,500 baud. Floppy drives cost extra, and so did the controller.
Storage
✓A microSD card full of disks, programs and cartridges. Disks are writable, and NitrOS-9 boots.
Loading a game
✕Type CLOADM, press PLAY, wait, and hope the volume knob was right.
Loading a game
✓Pick it from an on-screen list (F9 to F12), or have it start by itself at power-on.
Text
✕Lower case shows as inverse capitals on most models.
Text
✓A choice of fonts, with true lower case by default.
Keyboard
✕No Ctrl, no Alt, no function keys, and no auto-repeat.
Keyboard
✓A USB keyboard. Each key types what is printed on it, with auto-repeat.
Joysticks
✕Analog sticks that drift, on cables that tangle.
Joysticks
✓A USB gamepad works as both joysticks, and it can pick and start games too.
Screenshots
✕Point a camera at the TV and try not to catch the scan lines.
Screenshots
✓Print Screen saves a picture straight to the card.
Settings
✕None to speak of. What it does at power-on is fixed in ROM.
Settings
✓A text file on the card, for every game or just one, editable on screen.
Size and power
✕A keyboard-sized case with a mains cord, and 40-year-old capacitors inside.
Size and power
✓A 65 x 30 mm board, the size of a Raspberry Pi Zero, powered by a phone charger. A printable case shaped like a CoCo 2 is optional.
Upgrades
✕Want a CoCo 3? Buy a CoCo 3.
Upgrades
✓CoCo 3 palette and timer on the CoCo 2, and an experimental firmware that is a whole 128 KB CoCo 3.

The CoCo Zero is XRoar running on a Waveshare RP2350-PiZero. Bring your own ROMs, from hardware you own.
