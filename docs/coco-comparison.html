CoCo 2, CoCo 3, CoCo Zero

Tandy fixed some of it in 1986. The rest took forty years.

Tandy CoCo 2

1983, tired, beloved

Tandy CoCo 3

1986, the last and best

CoCo Zero

2026, on a 65 x 30 mm board

Picture
✕RF out to channel 3 or 4: fuzz, shimmer and a fight with the TV's tuner.
Picture
✓Composite and RGB outputs, sharp on Tandy's CM-8 monitor if you bought one.
Picture
✓A sharp digital picture at 60 frames a second on an ordinary TV, with the NTSC artifact colours recreated.
Sound
✕A 6-bit DAC squeezed through the same RF signal, so the picture's buzz comes along for free.
Sound
✕The same 6-bit DAC, and still no sound chip.
Sound
✓Clean digital sound over the same one cable, plus the Games Master Cartridge's three-voice sound chip built in.
Speed
✕POKE 65497,0 doubles the speed and scrambles the screen.
Speed
✓1.79 MHz with POKE 65497,0, and the picture holds.
Speed
✓Both high-speed POKEs work on the CoCo 2, up to twice as fast with a clean screen.
Storage
✕Cassette tapes at 1,500 baud. Floppy drives cost extra, and so did the controller.
Storage
✕The same cassettes, or the same floppy drive and controller, bought separately.
Storage
✓A microSD card full of disks, programs and cartridges. Disks are writable, and NitrOS-9 boots.
Loading a game
✕Type CLOADM, press PLAY, wait, and hope the volume knob was right.
Loading a game
✕Still typed: LOADM or RUN, then wait for the drive.
Loading a game
✓Pick it from an on-screen list (F9 to F12), or have it start by itself at power-on.
Text
✕32 columns, and lower case shows as inverse capitals on most models.
Text
✓True lower case, and 40 or 80 columns.
Text
✓A choice of fonts on the CoCo 2, with true lower case by default.
Keyboard
✕No Ctrl, no Alt, no function keys, and no auto-repeat.
Keyboard
✓Adds Ctrl, Alt, F1 and F2.
Keyboard
✓A USB keyboard. Each key types what is printed on it, with auto-repeat.
Joysticks
✕Analog sticks that drift, on cables that tangle.
Joysticks
✕The same analog sticks, drifting the same way.
Joysticks
✓A USB gamepad works as both joysticks, and it can pick and start games too.
Screenshots
✕Point a camera at the TV and try not to catch the scan lines.
Screenshots
✕Point a camera at the monitor instead.
Screenshots
✓Print Screen saves a picture straight to the card.
Settings
✕None to speak of. What it does at power-on is fixed in ROM.
Settings
✕Still fixed in ROM.
Settings
✓A text file on the card, for every game or just one, editable on screen.
Size and power
✕A keyboard-sized case with a mains cord, and 40-year-old capacitors inside.
Size and power
✕The same: a keyboard-sized case, a mains cord, and capacitors nearly as old.
Size and power
✓A 65 x 30 mm board, the size of a Raspberry Pi Zero, powered by a phone charger.

What came next
